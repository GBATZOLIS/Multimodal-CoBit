\begin{abstract}

We extend continuous bitstream diffusion (CoBit), originally developed for language modeling, to
joint and conditional multimodal generation using the same training and sampling protocol. Across
image--text, speech--text and protein sequence--structure modeling, each model is trained
separately from scratch and uses one input projection, transformer backbone, bitwise prediction
head and binary score-matching objective for all of its modalities. A single checkpoint supports
joint generation and the conditional directions included in training by changing which parts of
the bitstream are observed. We
first validate the approach on MNIST-Sum, a controlled image--text task pairing raw binary images
with equations encoded as text, achieving near-perfect conditional accuracy and high joint
consistency without learned tokenizers. \ifdefined\toyimageonly
We then apply the same protocol at larger scale to speech--text and protein sequence--structure
modeling.
\else
We then apply the same protocol at larger scale to natural image--text, speech--text, and protein
sequence--structure modeling. Models trained on CC3M and CC12M achieve zero-shot MS-COCO
text-to-image FID-30K scores of 18.48 and 16.59 with 20 and 32 denoiser evaluations,
respectively, while also supporting captioning and joint generation.
\fi Trained without text pretraining, a single speech--text model performs speech
synthesis, recognition, continuation, and unconditional co-generation. Jointly generated speech
and text achieve 3.5\% word error rate between the generated text and automatic transcriptions of
the generated speech, alongside generative perplexity of 133.3. In protein modeling,
one denoiser performs folding, inverse folding, and joint sequence--structure generation.
Jointly generated protein sequences and backbones (100--500 residues) reach a mean
self-consistency TM-score of 0.911 between the generated backbone and the predicted structure of
the generated sequence, with 69\% (69/100) scoring at least 0.9.
\bruno{In protein modeling, CoBit supports forward folding, inverse folding, and joint sequence--structure generation with the same denoiser. 75\% of generated backbones achieve a self-consistency TM-score of at least $0.9$ after one ProteinMPNN redesign and ESMFold refolding. }
Together, these results show that a protocol developed for language modeling learns joint
distributions with substantially different forms of cross-modal dependence, without a new
objective or sampling procedure for any domain.
\end{abstract}
