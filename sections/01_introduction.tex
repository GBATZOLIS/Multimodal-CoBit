\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Continuous generative diffusion models \cite{todo} have become an essential component of modern image, video, and audio generation pipelines \cite{todo}. The central idea of diffusion modeling is to learn the reverse of a corruption process (i.e., the forward process) that gradually transforms data into a pure noise distribution. While diffusion models have achieved their greatest success on continuous data such as images, recent years have seen growing interest in extending these methods to discrete data modalities, including language \citep{todo} and molecular sequences \cite{todo}. 

In this context, most approaches have focused on discrete forward processes, such as masking \citep{todo} or hard token flipping \cite{todo}, which progressively replace discrete tokens with other tokens until the original signal is lost. An alternative, first explored in \citep{todo}, is to embed the discrete tokens directly into a continuous space and then apply a conventional continuous diffusion process. This approach has seen renewed interest over the past year, with several recent works suggesting that continuous diffusion can match or even surpass the performance of its discrete counterparts \cite{todo}. Despite these developments, continuous diffusion approaches are still used primarily for continuous data, whereas discrete diffusion methods, or autoregressive models, remain the dominant choice for discrete sequences. This separation is particularly problematic in multimodal settings, where continuous data, such as molecular geometries, must be co-generated with discrete data, such as molecular identities. Doing so typically requires either integrating substantially different training pipelines within a single model or coupling separate models, both of which can hinder the learning and sharing of genuinely multimodal representations and information. Furthermore, this methodological segregation means that each modality must be tuned and calibrated within its own pipeline, requiring costly, case-by-case optimization and engineering rather than a unified modeling framework or common engineering standard.

In this paper, we introduce a modality-agnostic continuous bitstream diffusion (CoBit) pipeline capable of handling disparate multimodal co-generation tasks without the need for ad hoc calibration, beyond the choice of a digital vocabulary. The multimodal CoBit pipeline converts data from all modalities into sequences of bits, which are then corrupted with continuous Gaussian noise. As in previous work on language \citep{todo}, the diffusion process is calibrated using data-dependent entropy profiles, allowing the model to automatically adapt its noise schedule to the underlying data source during both training and inference \citep{todo}. As we show, the combination of a shared bitstream representation and entropy-based calibration makes it highly adaptable, enabling strong performance across a broad range of multimodal co-generation tasks with little or no domain-specific tuning.
