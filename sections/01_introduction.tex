\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multimodal generative models must reconcile data with very different structures. Images and audio
are commonly modelled through continuous diffusion, whereas language and protein sequences are
usually generated autoregressively or with discrete corruption processes
\citep{ho2020denoising,song2021score,austin2021structured,sahoo2024simple,wang2024dplm}. Existing
systems bridge these choices through modality-specific encoders, latent spaces, objectives or
prediction interfaces \citep{bao2023unidiffuser,tang2023codi,campbell2024multiflow,wang2025dplm2}.
These designs are effective, but each new modality pairing introduces another interface to design
and calibrate.

In this work, we ask whether a single generative interface can instead operate directly on discrete codes from
different modalities without domain-specific engineering choices. Bit sequences are the fundamental language of digital computers and therefore offer the most natural choice for a modality-agnostic encoding scheme. Autoregressive models are often inefficient in these settings, as an AR model needs to generate each bit sequentially. On the other hand, diffusion models are capable of highly parallel generation. Consequently, our starting point is continuous bistream diffusion \citep{method:cobit}, which writes discrete tokens
as binary codes and applies continuous Gaussian diffusion to their bits. We map each modality to
integer codes with a frozen tokenizer, place the codes in a shared binary vocabulary, and arrange
them in a fixed sequence template. Inside the denoiser, every position uses the same input
projection, transformer backbone, bitwise prediction head and loss. A partial-denoising objective
then trains one checkpoint to perform joint generation and every conditional direction included in
training: clean positions specify the conditioning signal, and corrupted positions specify what to
generate. Since we are interested in validating a more universal (i.e. modality agnostic) form of co-generation, the architecture contains no modality- or segment-specific parameters. 

Our main contribution is to demonstrate that continuous bitstream diffusion enables capable multimodal co-generation across diverse domains without modality-specific engineering or architectural design. By representing different modalities through a common binary interface, we show that a single denoising architecture and objective can learn their joint distributions and support both unconditional co-generation and conditional generation in either direction, without task-specific modules or fine-tuning. We validate this approach across image–text, speech–text, and protein sequence–structure modeling, demonstrating that a unified bitstream formulation can capture complex cross-modal dependencies across fundamentally different data types without tailoring the generative model to each domain.
