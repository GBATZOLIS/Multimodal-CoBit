\subsection{Image and text}
\label{sec:exp:imagetext}

Image and text test whether one generative interface can span very different surface structure.
CoBit flattens both into one 1-D bitstream and uses the same projection, transformer and bit
predictor for image$\rightarrow$text, text$\rightarrow$image and joint generation; direction is
specified only by which part of the stream is observed. We first make every direction exactly
checkable, then apply the same interface to natural images and captions.

\textbf{An exactly auditable control.} MNIST-Sum pairs four binarised MNIST digits
\citep{image:mnist} with their ordered equation and sum. It is included not because MNIST is
difficult but because every conditional and joint direction has a checkable answer, which
natural-image metrics cannot offer. Raw binary pixels and a deterministic 40-symbol, 7-bit text
code share one flat sequence with no learned tokenizer on either side; the denoiser receives the flat
bitstream and its 1-D position but no pixel grid or 2-D attention structure. A single
63.3M-parameter model trained for 500K steps reads and adds a supplied image with 97.90\% exact
accuracy, renders all four requested digits from a supplied equation in 99.98\% of samples, and
co-generates mutually consistent pairs from noise 96.85\% of the time
(Figure~\ref{fig:mnist:sum}). Held-out digit tuples give 97.92\% image$\rightarrow$text,
statistically indistinguishable from seen tuples. Raw visual bits and symbolic text bits therefore
form a learnable joint generative space under one modality-agnostic 1-D denoiser;
Appendix~\ref{app:image:mnist} reports classifier calibration, confidence intervals and the error
decomposition.

\textbf{Natural-image setup.} CoBit-CC3M (462M) and CoBit-CC12M (633M) are trained on 2.9M and
11.7M alt-text pairs \citep{image:cc3m,image:cc12m}. A frozen pretrained Open-MAGVIT2 codec
\citep{image:magvit2,image:lfq} supplies 18-bit image codes at $f16/f8$; text uses \texttt{o200k}
and one namespace bit forms a common 19-bit space (\S\ref{sec:method:multimodal}). The denoisers
are trained from scratch, without language or semantic-encoder pre-training, auxiliary alignment
losses or a staged curriculum. Evaluation on unseen MS-COCO \citep{image:mscoco} is zero-shot
(Appendix~\ref{app:image:protocol}). Besides FID we report CMMD \citep{image:cmmd}, a kernel
(MMD) distance between generated and real images in CLIP embedding space that is unbiased and
stable at small $n$ where FID is not; our variant uses ViT-B/32 embeddings and a median-heuristic
bandwidth, so its values are comparable across our rows but not to published CMMD figures.

\textbf{A shared bitstream is already a capable image generator.} Text$\rightarrow$image is the
strongest natural-image direction: CoBit-CC3M reaches zero-shot FID-30K \textbf{18.48} with 20 NFE
and CoBit-CC12M reaches \textbf{16.59} with 32 NFE. On the same CC3M corpus and protocol, this is
8.46 FID better than LAFITE without a CLIP text encoder. The codec accounts for only 0.59/0.20 FID;
the measured result is over 30{,}000 prompts, while Figure~\ref{fig:image:t2i-showcase} supplies
selected concrete examples. Broader published context is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:image:repro}.

\textbf{The same checkpoint supports every direction.} CC3M image$\rightarrow$text reaches CLIP
24.83 and RefCLIPScore 0.661, and unprompted pairs reach within-pair CLIP 25.67. Its CIDEr of 0.136
shows that fluent discrete text remains the weaker side, particularly without language pre-training
and under noisy alt-text. Step count is the only striking inference-time asymmetry: image metrics
prefer 10/16--20 steps, whereas captioning improves through 256; the sweep is deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:image:sampler}.

\textbf{Scaling helps the images and hurts the alignment.} Moving from CC3M to CC12M ($4\times$
the pairs, a better $f8$ codec, $\approx8.7\times$ the compute) improves every image-side
distributional metric (FID 18.48$\rightarrow$16.59, CMMD 18.41$\rightarrow$17.76) and worsens every
metric of image--text agreement: text$\rightarrow$image CLIP by 0.5--1.0 at every matched guidance
(Table~\ref{tab:image:headline}), image$\rightarrow$text CLIP by 1.85 / 1.89 at the two guidance
values tested for both models, captioning CIDEr $0.136\rightarrow0.057$ and GenEval
$0.249\rightarrow0.222$. About 0.4 of the 1.22-point headline CLIP gap is the different guidance of
the two operating points; the remainder is the model. We see two candidate causes and cannot
separate them with the runs we have. \emph{Data}: CC12M alt-text is noisier and less literal than
CC3M's, so the larger corpus teaches a better image prior but a looser image--text coupling; all
alignment metrics moving together is what this predicts. \emph{Token budget}: the $f8$ codec gives
CC12M 1{,}024 image tokens against 96 text tokens (CC3M: 256 against 48), so text falls from 16\%
to 8\% of every sequence under one modality-agnostic loss, halving both its share of the training
signal in joint examples and its share of the context that carries the condition in
text$\rightarrow$image. A controlled pair (same corpus at $f16$ and $f8$, or CC12M images with
CC3M-quality captions) would separate the two; we consider it the most important open question in
this section. GenEval
also still exposes weak attribute binding for both models (Appendix~\ref{app:image:geneval}),
motivating larger models, cleaner captions and text pre-training. The central result is unchanged:
once useful geometry is encoded in the tokens, one 1-D bitstream model supports conditional and
joint generation without modality-specific towers or heads.
