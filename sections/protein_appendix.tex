\subsection{Training and Sampling}
\label{app:protein:training}
\label{app:protein:noise}

For each residue we combine five amino-acid bits (most significant bit first) and thirteen LFQ sign bits. Sequence decoding maximises bit likelihood over the 20 canonical amino acids. All 8192 structural codes remain available. The frozen DPLM-2 decoder returns N/C$\alpha$/C/O coordinates. We train the 396.23M-parameter denoiser from scratch for 300{,}000 steps (Table~\ref{tab:protein:training}) and use the validation-selected checkpoint at step 245{,}000 for every task.

\textbf{Thirteen structural bits.} The DPLM-2 codec quantises a 13-dimensional latent vector by testing whether each of the 13 learned values is greater than zero. These bits inform each residue position with one of DPLM-2's $2^{13}=8192$ possible structural codes \citep{protein:dplm2}. CoBit preserves these codes by representing each one of them as a bit, making the conversion between a stored code and its thirteen bits exact, also preserving the codec's full vocabulary. The frozen decoder reconstructs backbone coordinates from the sequence structural codes, and for that reason thirteen bits are sufficient to encode each codec output, although this does not imply lossless encoding of arbitraty coordinates (the learned structural codec itself is lossy).

Figure~\ref{fig:protein:tokenizer} shows the 20-residue excerpt of a retained 100-residue sample as a sequence, backbone and matrix of bits. \textbf{Sampling configuration.} All protein results in \S\ref{sec:exp:protein} use entropic DDIM with $G=50$ noise-grid points, classifier-free guidance of 5 for conditional tasks, and no guidance for joint generation. The grid is built from the checkpoint's entropy profile (Appendix~\ref{app:method}) with weight $[\sigma^3/(\sigma^3+0.1^3)]\sqrt{e(\sigma)/\sigma^2}$. Noise levels range from 0.002 to 80 and we keep structure-noise scale=1. Stochastic sampling uses churn 10 and we keep noise multiplier fixed at 1, applied between the 10th and 90th percentiles of the entropy-based cumulative distribution.

\label{app:protein:metrics}

% Each task follows established protocols: for inverse folding, we generate four sequences for CATH target. For inverse folding, we generate four sequences per CATH target. Amino-acid recovery (AAR) averages residue identity over candidates, then targets. Only the first sequence is refolded while forward metrics average four predictions per CAMEO target, then targets. Joint generation uses 20 pairs at each length in $\{100,200, \,...\,,500\}$, retaining all 100 draws without quality filtering or replacement. Seeds are derived deterministically from campaign seed 20260911 and sample IDs and  none of the aggregate scores use reference-based candidate selection, MSA or retrieved homologues. This cohort has 76 distinct generated sequences (repeated sequences remain in the denominator). Each joint sweep setting uses the same 100 length/seed assignments.

\textbf{One-redesign protocol.} We follow the published PMPNN1 procedure of \citet{protein:simpledesign} by redesigning each generated backbone with proteinMPNN and refold that sequence with ESMFold. We use full N/C$\alpha$/C/O backbones and temperature $.1$. Only the first ProteinMPNN sequence (candidate zero) is refolded, without selection by refolding score. ESMFold uses the Hugging Face implementation with \texttt{num\_recycles=3}, an FP16 trunk and chunk size 128 (reduced to 64 or 32 only on memory exhaustion). Aggregate scores use no reference-based candidate selection, multiple sequence alignments or retrieved homologues \citep{protein:proteinmpnn,protein:esmfold}.

\subsubsection{Sampler selection and cost}
\label{app:protein:pareto}


% We sweep CFG $\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$, $G\in\{50,100,150,200\}$ and Karras fraction $\alpha\in\{0,.1,.2,.5,1\}$, with $\sigma_i=(1-\alpha)\sigma_i^{\rm entropic}+\alpha\sigma_i^{\rm Karras}$ and Karras $\rho=7$. We obtained 120 settings per conditional tasks and 20 joint settings. CATH validation used the 16 pure-entropic settings with CFG 2--5. After requiring full coverage and exact clamping, selection maximises success (scRMSD $<2$\,\AA{} or scTM $>.5$), scTM and pLDDT, then minimises scRMSD. We then maximise diversity and AAR, and we select based on lower cost.
We sweep CFG $\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$, $G\in\{50,100,150,200\}$ and Karras fraction $\alpha\in\{0,.1,.2,.5,1\}$, with $\sigma_i=(1-\alpha)\sigma_i^{\rm entropic}+\alpha\sigma_i^{\rm Karras}$. The reported configuration was selected on CATH validation from the 16 pure-entropic settings with CFG 2--5. After requiring full target coverage and exact clamping, settings are ranked by maximising success (scRMSD $<2$\,\AA{} or scTM $>.5$), scTM and pLDDT, then minimising scRMSD, maximising diversity and AAR, and preferring fewer steps. 

We observed that at $G=50$, increasing entropic CFG from 0 to 5 raises inverse scTM from .708 to .739 while diversity (one minus mean pairwise identity among four designs) falls from .392 to .145. Strict success does not improve consistently (Figure~\ref{fig:protein:cfg-evaluation}). The matched 100-pair joint sweep reaches its highest mean scTM of $.907$ at  $\alpha=.1$, $G=50$, versus $.899$ for the reported $\alpha=0$, $G=50$ configuration. Within that sweep, scTM rises from .899 at the reported configuration to .903 at $\alpha=.1$, $G=200$, which costs roughly four times as many denoiser evaluations (Figure~\ref{fig:protein:pareto}). All main results use the fixed 50-point entropic configuration.
